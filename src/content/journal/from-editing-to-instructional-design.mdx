Before any of this I was an editor. Fiction and non-fiction, for Random House Struik and NB Publishers, with an MA in English behind me and no plan whatsoever to end up in organisational learning.

People treat that as a career change. It doesn't feel like one.

## Editing teaches you where a reader falls off

A manuscript arrives and the author knows exactly what they mean. The whole job is finding the places where that meaning fails to survive the trip to someone else's head – the paragraph that assumes context the reader doesn't have, the argument that skips its second step, the chapter that is doing three jobs and none of them well.

That is the same job as designing a learning experience. A subject-matter expert knows their material completely. What they cannot easily see is the sequence a person who doesn't know it needs to travel, or the point at which they'll quietly give up.

## Structure is a form of respect

Good editing is largely invisible. You notice its absence – a book that sprawls, a chapter that won't end – far more than its presence.

Programme design works the same way. When the architecture is right, nobody in the room remarks on the architecture. They talk about the conversation they had, the thing they realised, the colleague they argued with productively. The structure did its work by getting out of the way.

The corollary is that structure is not bureaucracy. It's the thing that makes ambiguity survivable. The most interesting organisational problems arrive messy – competing stakeholders, unclear requirements, several definitions of success in the same meeting. The value isn't in pretending that mess away. It's in building enough structure that people can work inside it without drowning.

## What carried over

Three things, mostly.

An ear for whether a sentence is doing what it claims to. A tolerance for other people's first drafts, including their first drafts of an idea. And the habit of asking what a reader – or a participant, or a leader – actually needs next, rather than what the material happens to contain.
