Expanding a leadership initiative from eight African countries to thirteen sounds like a scaling problem. It isn't, quite. Scaling implies doing more of the same thing. What multi-country design actually asks is something harder: holding one coherent programme identity while letting it mean something different in each place it lands.

## Consistency is not sameness

The failure mode is a programme designed in one capital city and shipped everywhere unchanged. Participants recognise it immediately. The case studies are from the wrong markets, the examples assume infrastructure that isn't there, and the facilitator is explaining a context rather than working in one.

The opposite failure is just as common: thirteen versions so locally adapted that there is no shared programme at all, no common language between cohorts, and no way to say what the initiative actually developed.

What holds is a clear architecture with deliberately open sections. The learning outcomes, the sequence and the standard of rigour stay fixed. The cases, the examples, the guest practitioners and a good portion of the discussion are local by design, not by concession.

## Design for the facilitator you won't be

Across thirteen countries you are not in the room. Someone else is, often someone you have met twice.

That changes what a design document has to contain. It is not enough to specify what happens in a session. You have to make the intent legible — why this exercise sits here, what it is meant to surface, what to do when it surfaces something else. A facilitator who understands the intent can adapt in the moment. One who has only a script can only deliver the script.

## The unglamorous part

Multi-country programmes live or die on things nobody puts on a slide: whether the pre-work arrives in time, whether the platform works on the connection people actually have, whether the schedule respects a public holiday you'd never heard of.

The design work and the operational work are not separate disciplines here. A beautiful learning journey that assumes reliable bandwidth is not a beautiful learning journey. It's a document.
